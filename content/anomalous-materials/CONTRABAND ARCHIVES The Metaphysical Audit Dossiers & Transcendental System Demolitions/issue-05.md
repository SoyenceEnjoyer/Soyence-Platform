3. **Virtue Ethics & Teleological Realism:** Rooted in Aristotle and refined by classical theology, morality is defined by fulfilling human nature and cultivating character virtues (wisdom, justice, courage) aligned with our true purpose (*Telos*).

5. **Social Contract Theory:** Moral rules are pragmatic conventions negotiated by human societies to prevent lawlessness. Right and wrong are social constructs that change over time based on consensus.
6. **Divine Personal Realism (Orthodox Pre-Condition):** Goodness is neither an arbitrary decree nor a human convention—it is grounded in the **eternal, uncreated character and energies of God**. Moral obligations reflect the structural *logoi* embedded into creation itself.

#### Trap 1: Hume's Is-Ought Fallacy (The Chasm)
Scottish philosopher David Hume famously pointed out that thinkers routinely describe physical reality ("This is how nature works") and then quietly slip into moral commands ("Therefore, you ought to act this way").
* **The Rule:** **No collection of purely physical facts (Is) can ever produce a moral obligation (Ought).**
* Without a metaphysical bridge that defines human nature and purpose, you can never get from a description of physical matter to a binding moral law.

#### Trap 2: The Euthyphro Dilemma & Its Resolution
First formulated in Plato's dialogue *Euthyphro*, critics frequently use this classic dilemma against religious ethics:
> *"Is something Good because God commands it, or does God command it because it is inherently Good?"*

* **Horn 1 (Voluntarism):** If it is good only because God commands it, then morality is arbitrary. If God commanded murder tomorrow, murder would become good.
* **Horn 2 (Intellectualism):** If God commands it because it is inherently good, then "Goodness" is a standard higher than God, making God subordinate to an external law.
* **The Classical Orthodox Resolution:** The dilemma presents a **false dichotomy**. Goodness is neither an arbitrary external command nor a law above God. Goodness is God's very nature and uncreated energies. God commands what is good because His commands express His eternally consistent, loving character.

#### Trap 3: The Arbitrary Preference Trap of Secular Moral Realism
Modern secular thinkers often try to rescue objective morality without God by defining goodness as "Human Flourishing."
* **Why it collapses:** Who defines what "flourishing" means? Is flourishing defined by peaceful cooperation, or by ruthless competitive dominance?
* Without an objective *Telos* (design/purpose) grounded in metaphysics, "flourishing" is just a **subjective preference**. You have simply replaced the word "Good" with the word "Flourishing," leaving the underlying justification unproven.
