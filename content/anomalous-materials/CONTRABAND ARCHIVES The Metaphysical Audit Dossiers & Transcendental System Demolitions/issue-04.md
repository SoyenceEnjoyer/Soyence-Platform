Epistemology tackles the fundamental mechanics of human thought:
* **Justification:** What gives a human being the right to claim a belief is true rather than just an opinion, a guess, or a hallucination?
* **Origin of Ideas:** Does human knowledge originate in raw sensory experience, innate rational concepts, or divine illumination?
* **Limits of Cognition:** Are there **structural boundaries** to what the human mind can perceive and process?

For centuries, philosophers defined knowledge using the classical formula: **Justified True Belief (JTB)**.

$$\text{Knowledge} = \text{Belief} + \text{Truth} + \text{Justification}$$

To claim you "know" something, three conditions must align:
1. You must believe it.
2. It must actually be true in reality.
3. You must possess **adequate justification** (reasons, evidence, or grounds) for that belief.

The central battle in epistemology takes place entirely around point #3: **What counts as valid justification?**

#### Trap 2: The Problem of the Criterion
Formulated by ancient skeptics and refined by Roderick Chisholm, this problem asks a deceptively simple question: **How do you know your criterion of truth is correct?**


* To know if a statement is true, you need a reliable criterion/method to test it.
* But to know if your criterion/method is reliable, you must test it against known true statements.
* You cannot select true statements without a criterion, and you cannot select a criterion without true statements. Without a transcendental anchor, **autonomous human epistemology spins in a circle**.

#### Trap 3: David Hume & The Collapse of Empiricism
In the 18th century, radical empiricist David Hume delivered a hammer blow to secular philosophy by exposing the **logical flaw inside raw empiricism**:
1. If all knowledge comes from sense experience, we can only observe sequences of events (Event A happens, then Event B happens).
2. We never observe the invisible force called "Causation"—we only see correlation.
3. Therefore, an empiricist has no logical right to claim that Event A caused Event B, nor do they have a logical right to expect the future to resemble the past (The Problem of Induction).
* Hume demonstrated that pure empiricism **destroys science, cause-and-effect, and human rationality**, reducing all knowledge to habitual expectation.
