### The Primary Metaphysical Soil Types
Throughout human history, thinkers have proposed distinct metaphysical frameworks to explain ultimate reality. We can categorize these into **six primary "Soil Types"**:

```

                                        |

(Physicalism)  (Advaita/Māyā)  (Śūnyatā/Void)   (Mind & Matter) (Simple God)   (Triune Logoi)

```

1. **Physicalism / Materialism:** Reality is strictly physical matter, energy, and fundamental forces. Everything—including human consciousness, love, logic, and morality—is an **accidental, temporary byproduct** of physical particles interacting over time.
2. **Monistic Idealism / Pantheism:** The physical world of distinct objects is an **illusion** (*Māyā* in Advaita Vedānta). However, underlying the illusion sits an ultimate, reality: an undivided, singular Mind or Absolute Being (*Brahman*). All physical diversity is merged into a single cosmic consciousness.
3. **Illusionism / Radical Void / Anti-Realism:** Found primarily in Eastern traditions like Madhyamaka Buddhism (*Śūnyatā*), this view denies that any foundational substance exists—neither physical matter nor underlying divine consciousness. Reality has **no inherent essence**, permanent souls, or absolute grounds. Everything is an empty, interdependent flow of flux and illusion without an anchor.
4. **Dualism / Process:** Reality is permanently split between two co-eternal, distinct principles: mind and matter, or light and darkness (e.g., Cartesian Dualism, Gnosticism, or Process Philosophy). Reality evolves through the ongoing tension or interaction between these two forces.
5. **Classical Monadic Theism:** Ultimate reality is grounded in a **single, strictly simple, undivided transcendent Mind (God)** that created the physical cosmos out of nothing. The divine essence is completely simple, without parts or distinct internal relationships (e.g., Absolute Divine Simplicity in Neoplatonism, Thomism, or Islam).
6. **Triadic Personal Realism (Orthodox Pre-Condition):** Ultimate reality is grounded in a Triune God whose essence remains transcendent, but whose uncreated energies saturate and sustain creation. Creation is structured by a network of divine intentions (*logoi*) that grant **distinct, genuine identities** to particulars while binding them into a unified, intelligible whole.
